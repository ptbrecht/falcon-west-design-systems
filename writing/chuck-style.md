# Falcon West writing guide: emulate Chuck Yates (Old Man Coffee)

Source: https://collide.io/old-man-coffee (Chuck Yates / Collide).  
Goal: steal the **craft**, not the costume. We write insurance for Falcon West. We are not Collide, not PE Twitter, and not Chuck. We want his internet clarity, not his brand.

Corpus note (Sep 2026): ~22 posts. Typical length ~400–2,200 words. Sentence median ~10 words. Heavy first person + direct "you." Closer often: "Sip slow(ly), my friends."

---

## The one-sentence thesis

Open like a human who already lived it. Prove the pattern with receipts. Tell one specific reader what to do. Stop before the brochure.

---

## What Chuck does that we want

### 1. Cold open. Never thesis-first.
He does not start with "In today's rapidly evolving landscape." He starts mid-story, mid-confession, mid-joke.

Examples of his opens:
- "Good lord. I knew energy software could inspire some strong opinions…"
- "My daughter Charlie hit record on her phone before the coffee even landed."
- "I tell my kids the same thing every day: AI will not steal your job…"
- "In my 32 years in this business, if there's one thing I've learned, it's this…"

**FW move:** First sentence is a scene, a quote, a number that stings, or a client-shaped moment. The "what this article is about" lands in paragraph two or three.

### 2. Short sentences. Then one that lands.
Median ~10 words. Punch, then breathe. Occasional longer sentence for the turn.

**FW move:** Cut throat-clearing. Prefer "Homeowners still covers fire." over "It is important to note that a standard homeowners policy…"

### 3. Earned first person.
"I" is everywhere, but it is **receipts**, not vibes. Years in the seat, named people, named places, named failures.

**FW move:** Use Mike / Peter / Falcon West only when true. "We've seen clients with multi-million cyber losses" (Falcon Forward) beats "Industry experts say." Never invent a claim file.

### 4. One reader in the room.
He writes to "the engineer in the third row," not "stakeholders."

**FW move:** Pick the reader: the operator, the Amazon seller, the homeowner wiring closing funds, the new producer. Speak to that person. Drop "individuals and families seeking comprehensive solutions."

### 5. Digress, then snap back.
"Hold that thought." "Let me digress for a second." "Which brings me to…" Personal story earns the business point.

**FW move:** Spam email → wire fraud. Krystina "no risk" → OEE. BBQ / kids / typewriter equivalents for us: a closing table, a loss run, a Seller Central notice. Digression must pay rent.

### 6. Metaphor from lived life, not from a deck.
Mold in the house → custom home → AI partnership. Typewriter → word processor → AI writing partner.

**FW move:** Insurance metaphors should feel physical: wire that left, laptop that locked, well that kicked, certificate that got rejected. No "journey," "ecosystem," "leverage synergies."

### 7. Kill fairy dust.
He names the enemy: marketing bullshit, magic black box, consortium-speak, perfect twelve-month roadmaps.

**FW move:** Kill "Amazon insurance" (implies Amazon covers you), "full coverage," "peace of mind package," "best-in-class solutions." Say what the form does and does not do.

### 8. Titles that sound spoken.
"I'd Have to Be a Real Asshole to Say No." "Out Over Your Skis." "I Thought My Ex-wife Hated Me Until I Started Selling Enterprise Software."

**FW move:** Prefer spoken titles over SEO soup. SEO can live in the meta pack. Display title should still sound like a human.

### 9. Soft product, late.
Bill of Rights / Collide pitch arrives **after** trust. Often a short close, not a hard CTA.

**FW move:** Educational first. Product page / soft "talk through it" last. Especially newsletters: tease → click. Do not finish the sell in the email.

### 10. End on a line, not a paragraph of mush.
"Sip slow, my friends." "Time to get out over your skis."

**FW move:** One clean closer. Then contact. No three-sentence soft-close stack unless Peter asks.

## Structure template (Chuck → Falcon West)

1. **Cold open** (story / confession / sharp line)
2. **Bridge** (why this matters now; one hard number or receipt)
3. **Body** (2–4 scenes or gaps; each with a specific reader beat)
4. **Pattern** (what the stories share)
5. **Do / don't** (concrete, short)
6. **Soft next step** (optional product; optional call)
7. **Educational disclaimer** (one line, not a sermon)

## What we do NOT copy

| Chuck | Falcon West |
| --- | --- |
| Heavy cursing as flavor | Rare. Prefer blunt over crude. Insurance audience + brand. |
| Ex-wife / divorce bits as comedy | Off limits unless Peter is writing personal. |
| "Sip slow, my friends" signature | Not ours. Find our own closer if we want one. |
| Selling Collide AI | We place insurance. Soft, accurate, form-first. |
| "Galactic Viceroy" persona | We stay brokers. Warm, sharp, not meme-lord. |
| "Old man" / "listen up, kids" / oldest-guy-in-the-room | **Off.** Peter is not old. No fogey narrator, no generational lecturing, no "back in my day" cosplay. Earned experience without age theater. |
| Em dashes as rhythm | FW.com Insights: avoid em dashes (house rule). Energy: max two. |

Emulate **clarity and nerve**. Do not cosplay Chuck.

## Before / after (insurance)

**Brochure (bad):**  
"In today's digital age, personal cyber threats are more prevalent than ever. Falcon West is proud to offer comprehensive personal cyber solutions designed to protect what matters most."

**Chuck-craft, FW register (good):**  
"In 1978, spam was a computer demo on ARPANET. In 2024, the FBI's IC3 logged over $16 billion in reported internet-crime losses. Homeowners still covers fire. It was never built for a locked laptop or a fake closing wire."

**Brochure (bad):**  
"Operators should consider Control of Well coverage as part of a robust risk management strategy."

**Chuck-craft, FW register (good):**  
"A kick is an influx. It does not mean you lost the well. A blowout is when formation fluid hits surface and the well is out of control. OEE is for that day, not for the day everything went fine."

## Checklist before publish

- [ ] First sentence could not appear in a carrier brochure
- [ ] At least one named receipt (person, place, number, statute, podcast, IC3 figure)
- [ ] One clear reader (not "everyone")
- [ ] Digression pays rent
- [ ] No fairy-dust phrases
- [ ] Product appears late or not at all
- [ ] Sentences mostly short; one punch line per section
- [ ] Accurate (Cover/Insurance loop when coverage-critical)
- [ ] Em dashes within house limits
- [ ] Closer is one line, then contact
