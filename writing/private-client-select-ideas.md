## Homeowners and property

### Page: Homeowners Coverage
URL: https://www.privateclientselect.com/coverage/homeowners

- **Coverage topics / products:** Primary estates and seasonal retreats; multiple properties; restoration; craftsmanship and full-value thinking; wildfire, hurricane, and other natural-disaster preparedness; risk mitigation for difficult-to-place rural property.
- **Framing / headline ideas:** A home policy should understand the property’s design, materials, setting, and purpose—not just its square footage. Move from post-loss payment to a guided restoration experience. Preparedness is part of protection, especially in high-risk locations.
- **Audience cues:** Owners of primary estates, second homes, rural properties, architecturally distinctive homes, and homes in catastrophe-exposed areas.
- **Falcon West adaptation:** Lead with preserving what makes an accomplished client’s home irreplaceable: workmanship, continuity, and the ability to make sound decisions before a loss. Potential content: a property-by-property review for primary, seasonal, and remote residences; resilience planning that connects underwriting, maintenance, and emergency readiness.
- **Source-specific caution:** PCS describes a rural-home placement example involving fire-department access and mitigation work. Treat that as a case-study prompt, not a Falcon West placement result.

### Page: Coverage hub
URL: https://www.privateclientselect.com/coverage

- **Coverage topics / products:** Personalized property, automobile, fine art and collectibles, yacht, and excess-liability coverage; coverage coordinated around family needs and legacy.
- **Framing / headline ideas:** Build a protection program around the client’s world rather than buying disconnected policies. Each asset class deserves specialist attention, while the whole program should work together.
- **Audience cues:** Families with several high-value asset categories and complex personal portfolios.
- **Falcon West adaptation:** Use a “portfolio, not a pile of policies” explanation in Falcon West’s own voice, with a protection-first review of gaps, overlaps, deductibles, locations, and liability.

## Automobile and collector vehicles

### Page: Automobile Coverage
URL: https://www.privateclientselect.com/coverage/automobile

- **Coverage topics / products:** Agreed value; new-car replacement; luxury rentals; unlimited vehicles; collector cars, motorcycles, golf carts, and other specialty vehicles; OEM parts; repair-versus-cash-value decision support.
- **Framing / headline ideas:** A vehicle can be transportation, a collection asset, or both. Establish value before loss, preserve authenticity during repair, and keep the client moving while a high-standard repair is underway.
- **Audience cues:** Luxury-car owners, collectors, multi-vehicle households, owners of specialty vehicles, and clients for whom time and standards matter as much as price.
- **Falcon West adaptation:** Explore the difference between insuring a car and protecting an automotive asset: valuation discipline, restoration standards, storage/use patterns, household drivers, and a practical continuity plan after a major loss. Avoid promising OEM availability, “unlimited” capacity, or a specific settlement option without policy support.

## Fine art, collections, and valuables

### Page: Fine Art & Collectibles
URL: https://www.privateclientselect.com/coverage/fine-art-and-collectibles

- **Coverage topics / products:** Fine art, collectibles, private collections, worldwide movement, fire/theft/earthquake/flood/breakage, blanket and scheduled coverage, new acquisitions, specialist restoration, market-value volatility, transit and emergency planning.
- **Framing / headline ideas:** Collection protection is preservation work, not just a limit on a declarations page. A collection changes over time, travels, and faces different risks in storage, display, transit, and catastrophe preparation.
- **Audience cues:** Art collectors, owners of outdoor sculpture and modern art, collectors of vehicles, wine, jewelry, sports memorabilia, and other specialized assets; households with staff and complex logistics.
- **Falcon West adaptation:** Build a collection stewardship series: inventory quality, appraisal cadence, acquisition workflow, transit and storage, catastrophe plans, and trusted restoration relationships. Position documentation and preparedness as part of protecting cultural and emotional value—not as administrative chores.
- **Source-specific caution:** PCS lists specific claim thresholds, percentage extensions, and new-acquisition windows. Those are useful prompts for questions to ask a carrier, never Falcon West facts or universal recommendations.

## Yacht and watercraft

### Page: Yacht Coverage
URL: https://www.privateclientselect.com/coverage/yacht

- **Coverage topics / products:** Vessel, crew, tenders, onboard property, marine environments, newly acquired yachts/tenders, refit and yard-period complexity, seaworthiness, crew safety, storm preparedness, and loss prevention.
- **Framing / headline ideas:** A yacht policy has to follow the vessel’s actual life—from purchase and refit to crew operations and travel. Protection should account for the people, equipment, environment, and specialist repairs surrounding the boat.
- **Audience cues:** Yacht owners, multi-yacht households, crews, owners undertaking refits, and clients who travel across jurisdictions.
- **Falcon West adaptation:** Publish a “before launch / before refit / before storm season” checklist. Emphasize coordination among owner, captain, yard, surveyor, broker, and insurer; use “seaworthiness and continuity” as the protection-first lens.
- **Source-specific caution:** PCS’s automatic-new-vessel and refit examples illustrate issues to investigate, not coverage promises Falcon West should repeat.

## Family office and multi-generational planning

### Page: Family Office
URL: https://www.privateclientselect.com/family-office

- **Coverage topics / products:** Coordinated property and liability; one-policy administration; high limits; high-risk areas; international properties and pursuits; multi-location programs; legacy and generational continuity; dedicated advisor/service model.
- **Framing / headline ideas:** The family office needs one view across assets, generations, and geographies. Complexity is best managed through coordination, clear ownership, and an advisor who keeps the program current as the family changes.
- **Audience cues:** Family offices, multi-generational families, households with 50-plus locations or extraordinary total values, international property owners, and families with multiple decision-makers.
- **Falcon West adaptation:** Create a family-office insurance operating model: asset map, entity/ownership map, location schedule, liability architecture, international exposures, renewal calendar, and claims/escalation contacts. Speak to the family office’s need for clarity and discretion without claiming to replace its legal, tax, or investment advisors.
- **Source-specific caution:** PCS publishes a very large multi-location case example. Do not reuse its numbers or imply Falcon West has handled the same scale unless independently documented.

## Excess liability, reputation, and personal risk

### Page: Excess Liability Coverage
URL: https://www.privateclientselect.com/coverage/excess-liability

- **Coverage topics / products:** Worldwide personal excess liability; legal defense; personal injury; libel and slander; property damage/loss; uninsured/underinsured motorists; reputation-sensitive exposures; social-media and political-activity considerations.
- **Framing / headline ideas:** The largest personal-liability risk may be reputational, digital, or cross-border rather than visible on a property schedule. A serious program pairs financial protection with thoughtful defense and scenario planning.
- **Audience cues:** Public-facing or high-profile individuals, business owners, active social-media users, globally mobile families, and clients with substantial assets or reputational exposure.
- **Falcon West adaptation:** Develop a “personal liability beyond the umbrella” framework: assets, household drivers, aircraft/watercraft, domestic staff, boards and nonprofits, online speech, travel, and business/personal boundaries. Keep examples educational and avoid asserting that any particular exclusion is covered.

## Lifestyle services and proactive risk management

### Page: Risk Management Services
URL: https://www.privateclientselect.com/risk-management-services

- **Coverage topics / products:** Water-leak detection and prevention; resilient construction guidance; background checks; cybersecurity and family-data protection; wildfire preparation; private-collection management; inventory/documentation; emergency planning; transit assistance; dedicated account management.
- **Framing / headline ideas:** Risk management is a service layer that can reduce the chance or severity of loss. Prevention should be practical, discreet, and matched to how an accomplished household actually lives.
- **Audience cues:** High-value homeowners, families with staff, clients building or renovating, collectors, wildfire-exposed households, and families concerned about cyber and privacy risk.
- **Falcon West adaptation:** Organize content around “before the loss”: water, fire, construction, cyber, staff/household access, collections, and emergency contacts. Offer checklists and questions for the client’s existing specialists rather than making engineering or cybersecurity promises. A strong recurring series could be “one risk, one practical improvement.”

## Claims and risk themes to adapt

Across the useful pages, the strongest recurring claims/risk ideas are:

1. **Pre-loss intelligence:** Identify water, wildfire, storm, cyber, construction, staffing, and collection risks before they become a claim.
2. **Restoration as an outcome:** The client wants the right materials, craftspeople, specialists, and decisions—not merely a check.
3. **Continuity under pressure:** Keep the household mobile, the family office informed, and the collection or vessel moving toward recovery.
4. **Coordination across specialists:** Broker, carrier, adjuster, restorer, captain, architect, security/cyber partner, and family-office staff should know their roles.
5. **Asset-specific valuation:** Homes, vehicles, art, wine, jewelry, and yachts do not all behave like ordinary personal property; documentation and review cadence matter.
6. **Global and multi-location complexity:** Travel, international property, multiple residences, and multiple entities can create gaps if the program is managed in silos.
7. **Privacy and reputation:** For successful clients, confidentiality, legal defense, online exposure, and family data can be as important as the limit itself.
8. **Service proof:** “White glove” should be translated into specific actions, contacts, checklists, and decision support—not used as an unsupported adjective.

## Messaging patterns: useful, but make them Falcon West’s

- Move from **policy purchase** to **protection strategy**.
- Move from **asset list** to **the life and systems around each asset**.
- Move from **replacement cost** to **restoration quality, continuity, and informed choices**.
- Move from **reactive claims handling** to **pre-loss readiness plus calm post-loss coordination**.
- Move from **one household / one location** to **family, entities, staff, residences, collections, and travel**.
- Move from **generic umbrella language** to **a scenario-based review of personal liability and reputation exposure**.
- Use questions and checklists to demonstrate expertise; do not copy PCS’s slogans, visual hierarchy, case-study numbers, or service claims.
- Keep all limits, deductibles, exclusions, eligibility, response times, geographic availability, and carrier references subject to actual Falcon West documentation.
